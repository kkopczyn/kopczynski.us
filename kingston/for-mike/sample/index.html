Subject
The town that burned (and threw a party about it)
Preheader
Five things to do in Kingston this weekend, plus a new orchestra and why the stone houses are still standing.
Sends
Thursday, Oct 15, 2026, 4:00pm ET · covers Fri Oct 16 to Sun Oct 18

Hi neighbor,

The leaves on Academy Green have turned, the trolley has packed it in for the season, and this weekend Kingston remembers the day it was set on fire. Somehow that's one of the best weekends of the year. Here are five ways to spend it.

THE WEEKEND FIVE

1. The Burning of Kingston · Thu–Sat, Oct 15–17 · Uptown / Stockade On Oct 16, 1777, British troops landed at the Rondout and torched New York's first capital. This weekend the Stockade marks it with reenactments and history at sites across Uptown. It's year 249, so the 250th is next October. Check the schedule before you go, and wear shoes for cobbles. (Listing via Kingston Happenings.)

2. Fela Kuti Birthday Night at Tubby's · Fri, Oct 16, 7pm · 586 Broadway Living Language, DJ Busquelo and the Freakout Spot DJs play Afrobeat in Midtown's smallest loud room. Tubby's is tiny, so get there early or expect to dance in the doorway. (Via Tubby's calendar.)

3. The Pumpkin Express · Sat–Sun, Oct 17–18 · Catskill Mountain Railroad, Kingston Plaza A train ride on the old Ulster & Delaware line, the "Gateway to the Catskills," with a stop at a pumpkin patch. If you have kids under 10 this is the obvious one, and if you don't, it's still a train. Runs again Oct 24–25. (Via Kingston Happenings.)

4. Western Swing & Two-Step with King City Corral · Sat, Oct 17, 7:30pm · Tempo Arts, 29 Wurts St A hot western-swing band in the Rondout's best dance room. The listing says to come early for a free lesson, so two left feet are covered. (Via Kingston Happenings.)

5. Aziz Ansari: Hypothetical Tour · Sat, Oct 17, 7pm · UPAC, 601 Broadway Big-name comedy in a 1927 vaudeville palace. Check whether resale tickets are still around before you go. If it's sold out, Tab Benoit and Ghalia Volt are playing Assembly the same night. (Via Kingston Happenings.)

Also good: the Farmers Market on Wall St, Saturday 9–1, is still outdoors through November. Trivia at Rough Draft is tonight (Thursday) at 6.

NEW IN TOWN

Kingston has an orchestra. The Erehwon Ensemble bills itself as Kingston's first professional orchestra, and it debuts with Skylife & Voodoo Dolls at the Old Dutch Church on Sunday, Oct 25, at 4pm. A new orchestra in a bluestone church built in 1852 is something to put in your calendar now. (Via Kingston Happenings.)

FROM THE STONE One thing about Kingston you can see on your walk home.

After the British left on Oct 17, 1777, most of Kingston was ash, but the walls were still standing. The Dutch had built in stone, mostly local limestone, and stone doesn't burn. Families moved back inside the shells and put new roofs on them. That's why the Four Corners at John and Crown is the only intersection in the country with pre-Revolution stone houses on all four corners. Stand in the middle (carefully) and look for the scorch-era walls under newer rooflines.
